## 4. Test (iv): GF -> TTMn (both GFs at 100 Hz for 1 s; seeds 0-9)

| arm | side | GF (Hz) | TTMn (Hz) | following (TTMn / GF) | median latency (ms) | TTMn spikes timed | PSI (Hz) | DLM (Hz) | criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | left | 97.0 | 0.0 | 0.000 | - | 0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | - |

| ACG | right | 97.5 | 80.9 | 0.831 | 0.10 | 809 | 136.3 | 3.7 | FAIL |

ACG criterion (following >= 0.9 and median latency < 1 ms on both sides): FAIL. This checks the implementation against the property it was built from (prereg/12 section 7). It is not an independent finding.

**Post-hoc diagnosis (not registered).** The TTMn misses GF spikes for two reasons:
- A gap kick that arrives while the TTMn is refractory (t_rfc 2.2 ms) is lost. Brian2 holds the `(unless refractory)` variables during refractoriness; in this engine an addition to v in that period is not kept (checked on a toy network). The same holds for chemical input to g in every arm, R included (`tests/test_model_variants.py`, `test_input_during_refractoriness_is_lost`).
- The GF is a Poisson input node without a refractory period.

A dead-time counter therefore predicts a following of 1 / (1 + r_GF (t_rfc + 0.1 ms)): left 0.818 (observed 0.838), right 0.817 (observed 0.831). The registered threshold 0.9 cannot be reached at a 100 Hz Poisson GF without changing the refractory handling; nothing was changed.
